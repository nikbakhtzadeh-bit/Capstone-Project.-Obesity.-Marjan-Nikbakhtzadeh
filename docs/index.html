1 Introduction

1.1 Background

Obesity is a major public health concern associated with multiple demographic, dietary, and lifestyle factors. Understanding the factors associated with different obesity levels may contribute to a better understanding of behavioral and health-related patterns.

1.2 Research Question

To what extent are demographic characteristics, dietary habits, and lifestyle factors associated with ordered obesity levels among individuals from Mexico, Peru, and Colombia?

1.3 Research Objectives

The objectives of this study are to:

  1. Describe the distribution of obesity levels in the dataset.
  2. Examine demographic, dietary, and lifestyle characteristics.
  3. Evaluate associations between these characteristics and ordered obesity levels.
  4. Compare ordinal logistic regression models.
  5. Assess model assumptions and diagnostic results.

2 Methods

2.1 Data Source

The data are obtained from the UCI Machine Learning Repository: Estimation of Obesity Levels Based on Eating Habits and Physical Condition.

The dataset contains 2,111 observations and 17 variables.

2.2 Outcome Variable

The outcome variable is NObeyesdad, an ordered categorical variable with seven levels, ranging from insufficient weight to obesity type III.

2.3 Statistical Analysis

Ordinal logistic regression will be used to examine the associations between demographic, dietary, and lifestyle factors and obesity levels.

Model comparison, parameter estimation, and diagnostic assessment will be conducted.

2.4 Data Preparation

The dataset was obtained from the UCI Machine Learning Repository and imported into R using the readr package.

The dataset contains 2,111 observations and 17 variables. No missing values were identified during the initial data inspection.

The outcome variable, NObeyesdad, was converted into an ordered factor with seven levels, ranging from Insufficient Weight to Obesity Type III.

The variables Height and Weight were excluded from the primary regression models because body-size information is directly related to the construction of obesity categories. Including these variables could introduce target leakage.

3 Result

3.0.1 Descriptive Statistics

The analytical dataset consisted of 2,111 observations and 17 variables, including demographic, dietary, lifestyle, and obesity-related measures. The outcome variable, obesity level, was classified into seven ordered categories.

The distribution of observations across the seven obesity categories was relatively balanced, with category frequencies ranging from 272 to 351 participants. No missing values were identified in the analytical dataset.

The sample included 1,043 females (49.4%) and 1,068 males (50.6%). The mean age was 24.31 years (SD = 6.35), with a median age of 22.78 years and a range of 14 to 61 years. Most participants (81.8%) reported a family history of overweight.

Regarding dietary and lifestyle characteristics, 88.4% reported frequent consumption of high-calorie foods, while public transportation was the most frequently reported mode of transportation (74.9%). Most participants reported consuming alcohol sometimes (66.4%) and eating between meals sometimes (83.6%). Only 2.1% reported smoking.

3.0.2 Ordinal Logistic Regression Models

To investigate the associations between demographic characteristics, dietary habits, and lifestyle factors and obesity levels, cumulative link ordinal logistic regression models were fitted using the clm() function from the ordinal package.

Four nested models were specified to evaluate the incremental contribution of different groups of predictors. The outcome variable, NObeyesdad, was treated as an ordered categorical variable with seven levels.

  • M0 (Null model): Included no predictors.

  • M1 (Demographic model): Included age, gender, and family history of overweight.

  • M2 (Dietary model): Added dietary variables to the demographic predictors.

  • M3 (Expanded model): Further included physical activity and technology-use variables.

Model fit was compared using the log-likelihood, Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), and likelihood ratio tests for nested models.

3.0.3 Fitting the Ordinal Logistic Regression Models

Code 
# Load ordinal regression package
library(ordinal)

# M0: Null model
M0 <- clm(
  NObeyesdad ~ 1,
  data = obesity_data,
  link = "logit"
)

# M1: Demographic model
M1 <- clm(
  NObeyesdad ~ Age + Gender +
    family_history_with_overweight,
  data = obesity_data,
  link = "logit"
)

# M2: Demographic + dietary model
M2 <- clm(
  NObeyesdad ~ Age + Gender +
    family_history_with_overweight +
    FAVC + FCVC + NCP + CAEC + CH2O + SCC,
  data = obesity_data,
  link = "logit"
)

# M3: Expanded model
M3 <- clm(
  NObeyesdad ~ Age + Gender +
    family_history_with_overweight +
    FAVC + FCVC + NCP + CAEC + CH2O + SCC +
    FAF + TUE,
  data = obesity_data,
  link = "logit"
)

The four ordinal logistic regression models were compared using log-likelihood, AIC, and BIC. Lower AIC and BIC values indicate better relative model fit, while likelihood ratio tests were used to assess whether the addition of predictor groups significantly improved model fit.

3.0.4 Likelihood Ratio Tests

Likelihood ratio tests indicated that the addition of demographic predictors significantly improved model fit (M1 vs. M0: χ²(3) = 689.30, p < .001). Adding dietary predictors further improved fit (M2 vs. M1: χ²(8) = 577.18, p < .001). The inclusion of physical activity and technology-use variables also resulted in a significant improvement (M3 vs. M2: χ²(2) = 92.06, p < .001).

3.0.5 Model Comparison

Four nested cumulative link ordinal logistic regression models were fitted to examine the associations between demographic, dietary, and lifestyle characteristics and obesity levels.

Model fit improved progressively as additional predictor groups were introduced. The AIC decreased from 8213.63 for the null model (M0) to 7530.33 for M1, 6969.15 for M2, and 6881.09 for M3. Similarly, the BIC decreased from 8247.56 for M0 to 6988.53 for M3.

Among the four candidate models, M3 had the lowest AIC and BIC, indicating the best relative fit according to these criteria.

3.0.6 Regression Coefficients and Odds Ratios

Interpretation of Regression Coefficients:

The expanded model (M3) identified several significant associations between demographic, dietary, and lifestyle characteristics and obesity levels.

Age was positively associated with higher obesity levels (OR = 1.04, 95% CI: 1.02–1.05, p < .001). Family history of overweight showed the strongest positive association among the included predictors (OR = 9.55, 95% CI: 7.47–12.20, p < .001). Frequent consumption of high-calorie foods was also positively associated with higher obesity levels (OR = 2.17, 95% CI: 1.69–2.79, p < .001).

Among dietary variables, higher FCVC, NCP, and CH2O values were positively associated with obesity levels. Compared with the reference category of eating between meals always, the categories Frequently, no, and Sometimes showed different associations with obesity levels. Calorie monitoring (SCC = yes) was negatively associated with higher obesity levels (OR = 0.46, 95% CI: 0.30–0.70, p < .001).

Physical activity (FAF) and technology use (TUE) were also negatively associated with higher obesity levels (FAF: OR = 0.65, 95% CI: 0.59–0.71, p < .001; TUE: OR = 0.80, 95% CI: 0.70–0.91, p < .001). Gender was not significantly associated with obesity levels after adjustment for the other predictors (OR = 0.87, 95% CI: 0.73–1.02, p = .089).

These associations should be interpreted cautiously because the proportional odds assumption was not supported by the diagnostic assessment.

3.0.7 Assessment of the Proportional Odds Assumption

Model Diagnostics

The diagnostic assessment identified substantial violations of the proportional odds assumption in the expanded ordinal logistic regression model (M3). The nominal effects test provided evidence of non-proportional effects for several predictors, suggesting that their associations with obesity levels were not consistent across the cumulative outcome thresholds.

The scale effects test also identified significant scale effects for several predictors, indicating potential differences in the variability of the latent response across predictor values.

The supplementary multicollinearity assessment showed that VIF values were generally low, with no evidence of problematic multicollinearity among the predictors.

Although M3 demonstrated the lowest AIC and BIC among the candidate models, the violation of the proportional odds assumption limits the interpretation of its coefficients as common effects across all cumulative outcome thresholds. Therefore, the model should be considered an exploratory reference model rather than a fully adequate proportional odds model.

4 Discussion

The present study examined the associations between demographic characteristics, dietary habits, and lifestyle factors and ordered obesity levels using cumulative link ordinal logistic regression models.

The findings indicated that demographic and dietary characteristics contributed substantially to the prediction of obesity levels. In particular, family history of overweight showed a strong positive association with higher obesity categories. Age and frequent consumption of high-calorie foods were also positively associated with obesity levels. These findings suggest that both demographic background and dietary characteristics are relevant correlates of obesity classification in this dataset.

Several dietary and lifestyle variables were also associated with obesity levels. Higher values of FCVC, NCP, and CH2O were positively associated with higher obesity categories, whereas physical activity (FAF) and technology use (TUE) showed negative associations. Calorie monitoring was also negatively associated with obesity levels. However, these associations should not be interpreted as causal relationships.

The model comparison results showed progressive improvements in model fit as demographic, dietary, and lifestyle predictors were added. M3 had the lowest AIC and BIC among the four candidate models. Nevertheless, the diagnostic results indicated substantial violations of the proportional odds assumption. Consequently, the estimated odds ratios should be interpreted cautiously because the assumption of common predictor effects across cumulative outcome thresholds was not adequately supported.

4.0.1 Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings.

First, the dataset contains both real-world and synthetically generated observations. According to the UCI Machine Learning Repository, approximately 77% of the observations were generated using the Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE), while approximately 23% were collected through an online survey. Therefore, the sample may not represent the broader population, and the findings should not be generalized without caution.

Second, the proportional odds assumption was substantially violated in the expanded ordinal logistic regression model. Although M3 showed the best relative fit according to AIC and BIC, its coefficient estimates may not adequately represent associations across all cumulative obesity thresholds.

Third, some categorical predictor levels had very small frequencies. For example, the CALC variable contained only one observation in the Always category. Therefore, CALC was excluded from the primary dietary model to reduce potential estimation instability. Some other variables, including SMOKE and MTRANS, were also not included in the final expanded model.

Finally, the observational nature of the dataset does not allow causal conclusions. The reported associations describe patterns within the available data and do not establish that specific dietary or lifestyle characteristics cause changes in obesity levels.

5 Conclusion

This study examined the associations between demographic, dietary, and lifestyle characteristics and ordered obesity levels using ordinal logistic regression. Family history of overweight, age, dietary characteristics, physical activity, and technology use showed associations with obesity categories in the analyzed dataset.

The expanded model (M3) provided the best relative fit among the four candidate models based on AIC and BIC. However, substantial violations of the proportional odds assumption limited the interpretation of its estimates. These findings highlight the importance of considering model assumptions and data characteristics when analyzing ordinal outcomes.

Overall, the results provide an exploratory assessment of factors associated with obesity levels in the UCI dataset. Further research using representative population-based data and statistical models that accommodate non-proportional effects is needed to confirm these associations.

# Introduction

## Background

Obesity is a major public health concern associated with multiple demographic, dietary, and lifestyle factors. Understanding the factors associated with different obesity levels may contribute to a better understanding of behavioral and health-related patterns.

## Research Question

To what extent are demographic characteristics, dietary habits, and lifestyle factors associated with ordered obesity levels among individuals from Mexico, Peru, and Colombia?

## Research Objectives

The objectives of this study are to:

1.  Describe the distribution of obesity levels in the dataset.

The dataset contains 2,111 observations and 17 variables.

## Outcome Variable

The outcome variable is `NObeyesdad`, an ordered categorical variable with seven levels, ranging from insufficient weight to obesity type III.

## Statistical Analysis

Ordinal logistic regression will be used to examine the associations between demographic, dietary, and lifestyle factors and obesity levels.

Model comparison, parameter estimation, and diagnostic assessment will be conducted.

### Ordinal Logistic Regression Models

To investigate the associations between demographic characteristics, dietary habits, and lifestyle factors and obesity levels, cumulative link ordinal logistic regression models were fitted using the `clm()` function from the `ordinal` package.

Four nested models were specified to evaluate the incremental contribution of different groups of predictors. The outcome variable, `NObeyesdad`, was treated as an ordered categorical variable with seven levels.

- **M0 (Null model):** Included no predictors.

- **M1 (Demographic model):** Included age, gender, and family history of overweight.

- **M2 (Dietary model):** Added dietary variables to the demographic predictors.

- **M3 (Expanded model):** Further included physical activity and technology-use variables.

Model fit was compared using the log-likelihood, Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), and likelihood ratio tests for nested models.

### Fitting the Ordinal Logistic Regression Models

Four nested cumulative link ordinal logistic regression models were fitted to examine the associations between demographic, dietary, and lifestyle characteristics and obesity levels.

### Regression Coefficients and Odds Ratios

Interpretation of Regression Coefficients:

The expanded model (M3) identified several significant associations between demographic, dietary, and lifestyle characteristics and obesity levels.

Age was positively associated with higher obesity levels (OR = 1.04, 95% CI: 1.02–1.05, p \< .001). Family history of overweight showed the strongest positive association among the included predictors (OR = 9.55, 95% CI: 7.47–12.20, p \< .001). Family history of overweight, age, dietary characteristics, physical activity, and technology use showed associations with obesity categories in the analyzed dataset.

The expanded model (M3) provided the best relative fit among the four candidate models based on AIC and BIC. However, substantial violations of the proportional odds assumption limited the interpretation of its estimates. These findings highlight the importance of considering model assumptions and data characteristics when analyzing ordinal outcomes.

Overall, the results provide an exploratory assessment of factors associated with obesity levels in the UCI dataset. Further research using representative population-based data and statistical models that accommodate non-proportional effects is needed to confirm these associations.
